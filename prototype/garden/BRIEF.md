# Brief: the garden map, dithered

You're designing the **graph view** of a personal site: a whole-page map of every note and the links between them. The site is a calm "garden": every post and note grows its own flower, decoration lives at the edges, and the reading experience stays quiet. The owner liked the idea of a **dithered pixel plate** (in the style of https://autolith.rocks: ordered 4×4 Bayer dithering on canvas, small pixels, ink on paper), but the current attempt (variant E) "does not look good yet". Your job is to make it **fit the vibe, readable, nice and cool**.

## What exists (all in this folder; read, don't modify shared files, copy what you need)

- `graph.json`: the real data. 214 notes (`slug`, `title`, `stub`) and 360 directed links. One big cluster of 94 (mostly distributed systems, Czech titles), clusters of 27, 15, 15, 10, many small ones, and 23 isolated notes.
- `gen_garden2.py`: shared Python helpers for the board format. Read the top: `JS_BASE` (rng, grid, `specimen(seed)` gives each note its flower's petal/centre colour and Latin name, `mark(seed)` gives its one-line ASCII mark, `specimenRows`), `nav`, `FOOTER`, `rows_loop`, `component`, `page`, `PAGE_CSS`, and the colour constants. Seeds for notes are `'notes/' + slug`.
- `gen_graph.py`: data loading, `force()` (deterministic Fruchterman–Reingold; params `gravity`, `spread`), `snap()`, community/bed detection. Exec its prefix up to `# ---- variant A` to reuse, as `gen_dither.py` does.
- `gen_dither.py`: **variant E, the starting point.** It produces `jungle/project/GraphDither.dc.html`: an offscreen canvas rendered to a data-URL `<img>` with `image-rendering: pixelated`, 3-px cells, real-text labels positioned over it with collision avoidance, invisible buttons for hover/click, a panel with the selected note's flower, and a wildflowers list. Study it first.
- `shoot.js` + `runtime.js`: a local renderer for the board format. Usage:
  `node shoot.js <outdir> '<json list of {file, out, height, y?, scale?, state?, selector?}>'`
  with `state` like `{"hover": <node index>, "sel": <node index>}`. Node indices are positions in `graph.json`'s `nodes`; `replikace` is a good, well-connected note to test hover with. Look at your screenshots with the Read tool. **Iterate on what you see.**

## The board format (`.dc.html`): rules that bite

- One self-contained file. Template markup inside `<x-dc>`, logic in `<script type="text/x-dc" data-dc-script>` with `class Component extends DCLogic { renderVals() {...} }`, classic JS, no imports, no network except Google Fonts.
- Holes: `{{path}}` is a dotted lookup only. Loops: `<sc-for list="{{x}}" as="i">`. Conditionals: `<sc-if value="{{x}}">`. Events: `onMouseEnter="{{fn}}"`, `onClick="{{fn}}"`, with handlers returned from `renderVals()`.
- Never rely on multi-line static text (pre-drawn ASCII in the markup gets reflowed). Draw ASCII from data via `rows_loop`, or draw pixels on the offscreen canvas.
- State lives in `this.state` (`setState`); the existing component ticks `this.state.tick` every 250 ms when motion is on. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (the base already does).

## The design system (keep it)

- **Ground:** sand `#F1ECDF`.
- **Text colours:** ink `#1F2B23`, muted `#535F52`, canopy `#2B7349`, river `#1D5A8F` (links).
- **Decoration-only colours:** fern `#3F8A52`, moss `#5E8E4A`, mango `#A87400`, hibiscus `#C24E39`, orchid `#8A4F98`, lagoon `#2A7099`, rose `#B5476E`, earth `#7A5C42`, bark `#5C4636`.
- **Type:** IBM Plex Mono for structure and labels, Literata for reading and Latin names.
- **Feel:** calm, cozy, botanical, museum-like (specimen labels, a herbarium). Not busy. Readability beats spectacle, but it should be beautiful and a little magical.
- **A note's colour** comes from its flower (`specimen('notes/'+slug).petal[1]`). Keep that link, so a note looks the same here as in the herbarium.
